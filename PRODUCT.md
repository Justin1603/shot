PRODUCT.md — SHOT

1. The job
When I see someone I want to approach but freeze because I don't know what to say, I want to make the first move without having to come up with or say the opening line myself, so I can break the initial awkwardness and start talking to them naturally.
Who, by situation (not age or city): Someone who is physically around a person they're interested in talking to, wants to make the first move, but feels too shy or awkward to initiate it.
Today they hire: Their own courage, advice from friends, pickup lines/conversation starters online, a basic “hey,” or—most commonly—nothing and letting the opportunity pass.
What needs doing: Give me something I genuinely feel comfortable using to initiate the interaction.
How they want to feel: Less anxious about making the first move and more confident that they have an easy way into the conversation.
How they want to look to others: Cute, playful and confident—not creepy, desperate or overly rehearsed.

2. The switch
What they'd fire: Overthinking what to say, searching for an opener, asking friends for suggestions, forcing an awkward introduction, or doing nothing.
Push (what's wrong with today): “I want to talk to them, but I have no idea what to say without making this awkward.”
Pull (what your product promises): “Just find something I like and show them.”
Anxiety (what worries them about switching): “What if showing someone my phone like this makes the situation even more awkward?”
Habit (what keeps them where they are): Doing nothing is safer. There's no possibility of rejection or embarrassment if they never approach.
The one worry onboarding must remove: “This is going to feel weird when I actually show it to them.”
How I know (what they did, not what they said): The initial assumption test was run with Ronak, Vidi and Priyansh. All three actually used the interaction and showed a prompt to another person rather than only reviewing the concept.

3. The core flow
The story: Find something I'm comfortable saying, show it to them, and break the ice.
I see someone I want to approach but don't know what to say.
I open SHOT.
I choose what I want to do:
❤️ Make a move
😏 Flirt a little
🧊 Break the awkwardness
👋 Start a conversation
💬 Get to know them
😂 Make them laugh
SHOT gives me a deck of relevant prompt cards.
I vertically browse until I find something I'd actually show them.
I tap that card.
The card immediately expands to fill the entire screen.
SHOT displays “Hand them your phone.”
I show/hand my phone to the other person.
They read the prompt.
They choose one of two contextual responses: positive or negative.
SHOT shows the appropriate reaction.
I tap Done.
SHOT gets out of the way and we continue—or don't continue—the interaction in real life.
Things they do today: ~3–5 mental/social steps: notice someone → think about approaching → invent/search for something to say → build courage → approach or abandon it.
Things they do with my product: 4 core actions: Choose → Browse → Show → Respond.
What can go wrong
Step 3: User doesn't know which mode fits → what must happen: Mode names must describe clear intentions and all six should be immediately understandable.
Step 5: None of the five prompts feels usable → what must happen: The five free prompts must represent different tones/boldness levels and be strong enough to demonstrate SHOT's value.
Step 7: User accidentally opens a prompt → what must happen: They need an obvious way to return to the deck without triggering anything.
Step 9: Person A loses confidence and doesn't show the phone → what must happen: They can exit Show Mode without consequence.
Step 11: Person B isn't interested → what must happen: Every relevant prompt must provide an obvious negative response with no pressure to continue.
Step 11: Person B responds verbally instead of tapping → what must happen: Person A can simply close/end Show Mode.
Step 12: Negative response → what must happen: SHOT ends gracefully rather than encouraging Person A to try another prompt.
Next story (only once this one works end to end): Users can create an account, save favorite prompts and unlock the complete Premium prompt library.

4. Onboarding
First value (the moment it first does the job for them): The user reaches the prompt deck and sees a prompt they genuinely think, “I'd actually show someone this.”
The worry it removes: “I won't know what to say without making it awkward.”
From opening the link to the first value
User opens SHOT from a shared link/social post → immediately sees “Don't know what to say? Show them.” → removes: “What is this app supposed to do?”
User sees all six intentions and chooses one → removes: “Will this actually fit what I'm trying to do?”
Prompt deck immediately appears → first value: they find something they would actually use.
Login: Not in V1. Authentication does not improve the core Browse → Show → Respond loop and creates friction before first value.
What we don't ask on day one: Name, email, profile, photo, gender, who they're approaching, location, relationship status, boldness level, permissions, interests, personality questions, or a tutorial.
What we ask later, and when: Account creation only after saved prompts/favorites and Premium become real product features—not during the Build Sprint V1.

6. The riskiest guess
If this is false, the product is pointless: Someone who is too shy to make the first move will actually be willing to show a SHOT prompt to another person, and the recipient will engage rather than finding the interaction too uncomfortable.
How I tested it, and what happened: Ronak, Vidi and Priyansh were given the concept/prototype and asked to actually use it with another person. All three did so and reported liking the interaction.
What changed in the plan: The fundamental hand-the-phone mechanic remains. The build can now focus on whether strangers understand and complete the flow without explanation, rather than continuing to debate whether the interaction itself is viable.

7. Milestones
1. I can open a live mobile-first SHOT URL and see the six modes.
2. I can select any mode and enter its prompt deck.
3. I can vertically browse five curated prompts and move backward to previous prompts.
4. I can reach the sixth position and see a blurred Premium-locked card instead of another free prompt.
5. I can tap any available prompt and have it immediately expand into full-screen Show Mode.
6. I can hand the phone to another person and they can understand the prompt without me explaining SHOT.
7. I can select the positive response and reach the correct positive reaction state.
8. I can select the negative response and reach a graceful negative reaction state.
9. I can tap Done and exit the interaction cleanly without being pushed into another prompt.
10. I can see the complete funnel recorded: mode selected → prompt viewed → Show Mode opened → response → SHOT completed.
11. I can give the live URL to someone who has never seen SHOT and watch them complete the core flow on their phone without my help.
Last: I can close SHOT, reopen it, and the required analytics/event data is still persisted in Convex.
